Annual edition: Education, human services, health care in Texas

Public-record example using Hillcrest Foundation's reported grantees. Prepared October 6, 2026.

This edition covers five things, each dated to its own source:

  • What your grantees reported, from their latest two public IRS filings

Filings run about two years behind, so read each section against its date. Every section names its sources and limits underneath it.

Within one filing each kind of person counts once, at the largest number given; kinds are not added together. One large grantee can swing a total, so the up and down counts are the steadier read. Grantees filing a 990-N postcard report no programs. Filings run one to two years behind.

What the foundations beside you took on

Foundations beside you are the 75 private foundations that funded two or more of your grantees in 2021 and 2022. In 2023 they made 588 grants, $25,786,499 in total, typically $15,000, to nonprofits in Texas with budgets of $100,000 to $10,000,000 that they had not funded in the two years before.

Sources and limits: private-foundation grants itemized on IRS Form 990-PF and tied to an organization; a budget is the nonprofit's total expenses as recorded with its grants. As of the 2023 filing year.

Texas, as seen in filings

Of the 3,955 nonprofits in Texas with budgets of $100,000 to $10,000,000 that received a visible private-foundation grant in 2023, 3,343 (85%) received more than half of that foundation support from one foundation.

Sources and limits: foundation support only, from private-foundation grants itemized on IRS Form 990-PF and tied to an organization; government, individual and earned revenue are not in it. As of the 2023 filing year.
