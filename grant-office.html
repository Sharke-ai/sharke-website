Why waiting is already late

Next year's funding is being decided right now.

The funders who will write checks next year are building those relationships this cycle. The programs renewing next year are reviewing who fits now. None of it waits for the moment you decide you need the money. By then the cycle is underway and the room is full.

Roughly 70% of foundations do not accept unsolicited proposals. That money goes where a funder already knows the organization. The relationship that puts you in that group forms months before the grant exists, not the week you apply. Sharke surfaces the invite-only funders worth building toward now, so the door is open before you need it. And the room is getting more crowded: 87% of foundation leaders report rising demand for the grants they give.

Federal programs freeze and priorities shift, and a grant you won before is no longer a safe bet to win again. When you need to replace that funding fast, there is no fast. The pipeline that catches the loss is the one you started before it happened. Sharke verifies whether a program is funding this cycle and whether you still fit what it funds now, before anyone spends the 50-plus hours a federal application takes.

Before you decide

When can we start?

Today. Start below, complete the ten-minute intake, and your office starts the same business day. Your first grants come back verified within the first week. Prefer to run it yourself? Self-serve is open on its own page: the full workspace that finds, verifies, drafts, and tracks, for $159 a month.

How do we pick the right tier?

By how much of your mission income comes from grants: about half is $249, three-quarters is $369, essentially all is $459. Pick the one nearest to what you think. If the first month reads differently, we show you the evidence and you decide.

What if we already have a grant writer?

Keep them. Sharke becomes the research and verification layer underneath: they receive verdicts with sources and drafts to shape, and their hours move from research to voice and relationships. And before anything goes out, we will still red-team the application against the funder's own record.

How much of our time does it take?

Plan on about an hour a month: reviewing verdicts and drafts, sharing your core materials in week one, taking an introduction when a funder asks for one, and clicking submit where a funder's portal requires your organization's own account. Those are the only parts we cannot do for you.

Do we need a call before starting?

No. The intake covers what the setup call used to, and a 15-minute kickoff is included in your first week if you want one. If you have a scope question first, book the call: the same 15 minutes, before instead of after.

What if we have not identified any grants yet?

That is the common starting point. Sharke finds and qualifies the funders that back organizations like yours, so you begin from a verified shortlist instead of a blank page.

What happens when staff turn over?

Nothing stops. The average development professional stays around 16 to 18 months, and the median vacancy runs six months (CompassPoint and the Haas, Jr. Fund, UnderDeveloped). With the grant office plan, the pipeline, the verdicts, and the funder relationships live with Sharke, so a departure does not take next year's funding with it.

Is this AI, or is a person involved?

Both. Sharke does the research and drafting, and every verdict is built from documented funder filings, 990s, award history, and eligibility, not AI guesses. With the grant office plan, the Sharke team manages the work for you.

Do we have to sign a contract?

No. It is month to month, and you can cancel anytime. To cancel, reply to any Sharke email; it takes effect at the end of the current period.
