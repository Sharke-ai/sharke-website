What the foundation grant history tells us

These figures come from grants that private foundations reported giving to nonprofits in IRS Form 990-PF tax filings.

1. A long funder list can hide reliance on one foundation.

The education-advocacy organization has 23 recorded foundation funders, yet one provided 65% of its recorded foundation support. The same concentration appears in three organizations with shorter lists.

Implication for the funding plan

Track the dollars expected from each funder, not just the number of names. A plan that repeats this concentration needs replacement prospects if its largest source does not renew.

2. Literacy and youth transition have fewer visible foundation relationships.

2 and 3 recorded funders

Both organizations report less than $1m in revenue. Their largest foundations provided 71% and 56% of recorded foundation support, respectively.

Implication for these nonprofits

Separate the plan for retaining existing support from the plan for adding new funders. More applications to the same small group would preserve the same dependence.

3. Peer connections can reach beyond the same program area.

4 shared foundations

Four foundations supported both educational services and food education.

Implication for peer learning

These two nonprofits have a concrete reason to compare what those foundations funded and how the relationships began. Useful introductions may cross program categories.
