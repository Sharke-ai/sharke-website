For your next planning discussion

Key findings from this historical sample, not a current funding forecast.

  • Visible foundation funding reaches a subset of the field. Of 4,745 nonprofits in the sample's Texas education, human-services and health area, 38% had a visible private-foundation grant in 2021 to 2023. Other funding sources are outside this measure.
  • Foundation support is concentrated. Among the 3,955 qualifying Texas nonprofits with a visible private-foundation grant in 2023, 85% received more than half of that foundation support from one foundation. This does not describe their total revenue.
  • Needs differ across places. The 2020 to 2024 survey estimates provide context for discussion alongside 2023 funding records. They do not establish unmet demand or an appropriate funding allocation by themselves.

Implications and next questions

Foundations: where would additional grantee support or relationship development help? Associations: which members need targeted assistance, and what evidence would help a sponsor decide to support them?

Before choosing priorities, confirm current local conditions, the populations each organization serves and the funding sources missing from these records. Reported activities are not independently measured outcomes.

What the foundations beside you took on

Foundations beside you are the 75 private foundations that funded two or more of your grantees in 2021 and 2022. In 2023 they made 588 grants, $25,786,499 in total, typically $15,000, to nonprofits in Texas with budgets of $100,000 to $10,000,000 that they had not funded in the two years before.

Sources and limits: private-foundation grants itemized on IRS Form 990-PF and tied to an organization; a budget is the nonprofit's total expenses as recorded with its grants. As of the 2023 filing year.
