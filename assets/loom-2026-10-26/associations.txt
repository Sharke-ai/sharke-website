Your members need help funding their missions. Make that part of membership. Here is what a nonprofit's funding pipeline can look like.

Sharke supports a group of five or ten nonprofits for a year. Within ten business days of the agreed start, you receive an initial brief. It shows how much funding each nonprofit needs and what information still needs checking. It also lists grants open now that each nonprofit could pursue, with amounts and deadlines.

Each nonprofit gets a funding pipeline: a plan of grants to pursue and funders to approach. We deliver the plan for each quarter during the month before it starts. We keep deadlines and next steps up to date throughout the year. Each nonprofit provides its program information and writes and submits its own applications.

This sample shows ten nonprofits side by side. Your first progress report arrives at month three. It compares pipeline progress with each nonprofit's target and shows applications pursued and grants received separately. You can show your board what members have built and where further support is needed.

At month six, we review progress with you and the participating nonprofits. We decide which funding gaps to focus on, which grants to pursue next, and which funders to contact. Support continues, with progress reports at months nine and twelve.

Members get practical funding support and skills they can keep. We work with each team to calculate the pipeline its funding goal requires. We review grant and funder fit together and help determine appropriate amounts to request. The goal is to finish the year better able to make funding decisions and manage that work on their own.

The annual report looks across your geographic and programmatic footprint. It helps identify where nonprofits may need more funding options, especially if a major funder changes its priorities or reduces support. Nonprofits can use that to consider other sources. Your team can use it in conversations about where more funding options are needed.
