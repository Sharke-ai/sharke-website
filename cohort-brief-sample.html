Sample cohort brief

What a cohort of 10 nonprofits would look like.

This is the brief you would receive: every organization in your cohort on one page, measured the same way, so you can show your board and funders where each one starts and, a year later, what changed.

Sample. The figures come from the public records of 10 real nonprofits, with their names removed. The one exception is the three "built" columns in the cohort table in section 1, which are an illustration. Your cohort's numbers will be its own.

1. The before-and-after you report

For every organization, you see how big a pipeline its funding needs and how much of it is built, at the start of the year, at month six, and again at the end. Every organization is measured the same way, so you can compare them on one page.

In this sample, 23% of the funders on the cohort's combined list could not make these organizations a grant at all. At 20 to 30 hours per application, that is 460 to 690 hours for every 100 applications built from an unscreened list.

The 20 to 30 hours is our planning figure for one application, not a measurement of any cohort.

5. The starting line

This is your baseline. At month six and again at the end of the year, we run the brief for the same 10 organizations. The change in each one's pipeline, against what its funding needs, is what you report to your board and funders.
