When a nonprofit you fund loses another funding source, its work can be interrupted. A funding pipeline helps it plan other options before that happens.

Sharke supports a group of five or ten nonprofits for a year. Within ten business days of the agreed start, you receive an initial brief. It shows how much funding each nonprofit needs and what information still needs checking. It also lists grants open now that each nonprofit could pursue, with amounts and deadlines.

Each nonprofit gets a funding pipeline: a plan of grants to pursue and funders to approach. We deliver the plan for each quarter during the month before it starts. We keep deadlines and next steps up to date throughout the year. Each nonprofit provides its program information and writes and submits its own applications.

The organizations and figures in this sample report are fictional. Your first progress report arrives at month three. It shows the grants and funders being pursued, deadlines, next steps, and funding still needed.

At month six, we review progress with you and the participating nonprofits. We decide which funding gaps to focus on, which grants to pursue next, and which funders to contact. Support continues, with progress reports at months nine and twelve.

Over the year, each nonprofit learns how to work out how large its funding pipeline needs to be to meet its funding goal. Its team also gets practice choosing grants to pursue, planning applications, and following up with funders. The goal is to finish the year better able to manage that work on its own.

You also receive an annual report to help with budget and strategy planning. It brings together funding patterns, local needs, and the activities nonprofits have reported.
