What the cohort reveals about foundation support

Sample cohort report

Education is well represented, but the support is uneven.

Half of the organizations shown work in education. Yet college access and educational services have the broadest recorded foundation networks in the cohort, while literacy has the narrowest. A single education total would hide that difference: visibility of foundation support varies sharply within the same priority area.

The cohort reaches many foundations, but shares few of them.

Across the eight organizations shown, 152 foundations appear in the award records. Only seven funded more than one organization in this group. This suggests the participants bring largely distinct funding networks. A funder familiar to one nonprofit may be a new lead for another, but a shared cohort does not imply a shared set of suitable funders.

Some funding connections cross program boundaries.

Four foundations supported both the educational-services and food-education organizations. Another supported both media and youth transition. These connections point to giving patterns that a single program label can miss. They give nonprofits a concrete starting point for exploring funders beyond their usual category and comparing the work those funders have supported.
