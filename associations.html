Training helps members plan. But who keeps the funding work moving? Here is what a nonprofit's funding pipeline can look like.

Sharke supports a group of five or ten nonprofits for a year. Within ten business days of the agreed start, you receive an initial brief. It shows how much funding each nonprofit needs and what information still needs checking. It also lists grants open now that each nonprofit could pursue, with amounts and deadlines.

Each nonprofit gets a funding pipeline: a plan of grants to pursue and funders to approach. We deliver the plan for each quarter during the month before it starts. We keep deadlines and next steps up to date throughout the year. Each nonprofit provides its program information and writes and submits its own applications.

The organizations and figures in this sample report are fictional. Your first progress report arrives at month three. It shows the grants and funders being pursued, deadlines, next steps, and funding still needed.

At month six, we review progress with you and the participating nonprofits. We decide which funding gaps to focus on, which grants to pursue next, and which funders to contact. Support continues, with progress reports at months nine and twelve.

Over the year, each nonprofit learns how to work out how large its funding pipeline needs to be to meet its funding goal. Its team also gets practice choosing grants to pursue, planning applications, and following up with funders. The goal is to finish the year better able to manage that work on its own.

You also receive an annual report to help with budget and strategy planning. It brings together funding patterns, local needs, and the activities nonprofits have reported.

Sharke handles enrollment and works with the organizations. You send the introduction and help make the connections. Recommendations are reviewed before they reach the team, with evidence and uncertainty made clear.

What does the annual edition include?

It covers new grantees of the foundations beside you, concentration of foundation support among organizations of your members' size, where foundation money is thin, local need, the work organizations reported, and organizations to explore beyond the supplied list. Reported activities are not presented as independently measured outcomes.

Foundations beside you are those that funded two or more of your members in the two years before. A new grantee is one a foundation had not funded in those two years. Filing dates are shown because public records lag current activity.

Who built Sharke

As a strategy consultant and investor, Collin Brown helped major American and European companies decide where to put their time and money: putting numbers and timing behind each opportunity, testing the evidence, and building a plan that still holds if the largest opportunity falls through. He also worked as a technology executive.

He built Sharke to bring that discipline to nonprofit funding: a pipeline capable of funding the mission, with each grant checked before your team commits the hours.

His grant wins include more than $1 million in federal awards across several grants. He served in the White House Office of Management and Budget and holds a Wharton MBA.
